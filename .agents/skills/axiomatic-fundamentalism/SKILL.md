# Axiomatic Fundamentalism

Applies the strict Axiomatic Fundamentalism framework to evaluate or structure scientific papers. It enforces a brutally logical writing style that treats the logical structure of a paper like an English version of Stokes' Theorem: the boundary (explicitly stated axioms and modelling choices) must strictly and entirely determine the interior (all derived consequences and corollaries), with absolutely no external leakage or "midstream imports."

## When to Use

  - To author or restructure a scientific paper using specific, brutally logical templates.
  - To review a document for strict logical traceability.
  - To extract or apply the authoring protocol and reviewer rubric.

## Minimal Author Templates

When drafting or restructuring text, you MUST use these exact templates to ensure traceability:

**Axiom**

> Axiom [ID] ([Name]). [Formal statement of Axiom].

**Immediate consequence**

> Consequence [ID]. [Statement]. Proof sketch. From [Axiom ID] by [mathematical steps only].

**Modelling choice**

> [Scope: ...] Choice [ID]. We define [objects/sets/constraints] to be... [Rationale: clarity only].

**Corollary**

> Corollary [ID]. [Statement]. Derivation. From [Consequence ID] and [Choice ID] by [algebra/calculus/variation]; no external postulates.

## Core Concepts (The Boundary and Interior)

  - **The Boundary (Premises):** Axioms, pure mathematics/logic, and explicitly declared modelling choices (definitions, units, geometries, cost functionals).
  - **The Interior (Derivations):** Immediate consequences and corollaries derived *only* from the boundary via mathematics.
  - **The Vacuum (Disallowed):** No external "laws," no tacit empirical rules, no undeclared absolute properties, and no defensive essays about plausibility.

## Workflow

### 1. Document Profiling

Read the target document. Identify the stated axioms and explicit modelling choices.

### 2. Apply Reviewer Rubric (If Reviewing)

Evaluate the target against these binary checks:

1.  Are axioms stated once, precisely, with symbols defined and scope fixed?
2.  Do immediate consequences follow from the axiom(s) and mathematics only?
3.  Are modelling choices declared explicitly (not smuggled as postulates)?
4.  Are corollaries proved without importing external laws?
5.  Do demonstrations instantiate earlier steps only?
6.  Are falsification items tied to specific derived statements?
7.  Are labels unique and references precise; is notation/units consistent?
8.  Are relational foundations (distinction precedes relation, no absolutes) respected?

## Gotchas

  - **Information Theory is not Physics:** Do not confuse information-theoretic measures with physical laws; they are modelling warnings.
  - **Relational Only:** Ensure all models are purely relational (no absolute, undeclared properties).
  - **No Retrofitting:** Reject any derivations that alter axioms or smuggle new premises to force a target result after derivations begin.
