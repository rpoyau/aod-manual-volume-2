                                         288 Lv


   288 Lv was first reported in 2025 by Oganessian et al. in “Investigation of reactions

with 50 Ti and 54 Cr for the synthesis of new elements” (2025Og03). A 289.9 MeV
54 Cr beam from the DC280 cyclotron at the SHE Factory at the Flerov Laboratory

for Nuclear Reactions (FLNR) of the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (JINR) in
Dubna, Russia impinged on rotating 0.70 mg/cm2 238 U targets. Evaporation residues
from the reaction 238 U(54 Cr,4n) were separated with the gas filled separator DGFRS-
2 and identified by their correlated α-particle emission and subsequent fission in two
double-sided strip detectors. “The energies of α particles or SF fragments and decay
times of nuclei in the decay chains of 288 Lv observed in the 238 U+54 Cr experiment are
shown in Fig. 1... Thus, in this experiment, a new isotope 288 Lv was registered for the
first time,...”

              al., Phys. Rev. C 112, 014603 (2025).
