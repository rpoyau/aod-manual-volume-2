\section{An All-Element Inner-Recurrence Campaign}
\label{manual2:all-element-campaign}

\paragraph{Setup.}
The campaign retains the 118 element reference records and the 27 curated
isotope/state records of \secref{manual2:isotope-survival}. Their union contains
135 distinct pairs \((Z,A)\). The 145 source records remain separate: an
unspecified reference selection, an assigned ground state and a tentative isomer
are different inputs even when their nucleon counts coincide. A preparation map
must distinguish their constructed states before different dynamics can be
attributed to that distinction.

For the declared neutral inventory, set \(N=A-Z\) and \(E=Z\). Construct
distinct proton-facing \(3{:}3\), neutron-facing \(3{:}4\) and electron-facing
\(1{:}2\) recurrent supports, each with two retained formation returns.
Their ordered list induces a directed ring with one incoming neighbour per
constituent. This adjacency is an application preparation. The ring label alone
does not establish the admitted fractal field ring gate of
\mainsection{subsec:fractal-field-ring-gate}.

Each inner scope maps to the proposed common outer scope. The independent outer
trace and certificate then close the proposal using
\mainequation{eq:fusion-closure}. The generic construction identifies retained
support and lineage; identifying an electronic or nuclear preparation requires
an additional state correspondence. In particular, hydrogen-1 has \(N=0\)
and a valid proton/electron inventory.

\paragraph{Derivation.}
The enumerated outward occurrences of this construction are
\begin{equation}
B=30Z+68N+4E,
\qquad RD_{\mathrm{outer}}=2B+1.
\label{eq:manual2-campaign-support}
\end{equation}
The final term counts the mandatory hinge. These constructed support quantities
retain their own types; the static ledger's conditional support sum remains
defined by its earlier model.

Let \(R_i^{(r)}\) be the signed response already committed to constituent \(i\)
before round \(r+1\), and let \(D_i^{(r)}\) be its directional duration from
the last two committed incoming contacts. The declared whole-return orientation
kernel and traversal pacing are
\begin{align}
w_i^{\pm}&=1+\max(0,\pm R_i^{(r)}),&
P_i^{\pm}&=\frac{w_i^{\pm}}{w_i^++w_i^-},
\label{eq:manual2-campaign-orientation}\\
d_i^{(r+1)}&=RD_i+D_i^{(r)}+\max(0,R_i^{(r)}).
\label{eq:manual2-campaign-pacing}
\end{align}
The orientation selects a complete continuation recipe; its H1 traversals,
hinge and return are subsequently executed and independently certified. The
two-outcome recipe selection is distinct from claiming two alternatives at
every atomic traversal.

A genuine inner reclosure emits a returned-current support to its neighbour.
For source orientation \(\sigma_j\) and the receiver-minus-sender recurrence
lag \(\ell_{i|j}\) bound at emission, the application assigns directional
reflection counts \((\max(0,\ell_{i|j}),\max(0,-\ell_{i|j}))\). The first
count supplies the additional reflected extent of the directed support trace.
This is the declared bridge from recurrence lag to reflection coordinates;
native admission validates the resulting support rather than deriving this
bridge. The last two committed supports then supply \(D_{i|j}\), and the
application commits
\begin{equation}
R_i^{(r+1)}=\sigma_jD_{i|j}\ell_{i|j}.
\label{eq:manual2-campaign-response}
\end{equation}
The opposite view carries the negative contribution. The contributing contact,
its prefixes and its nonnegative burden remain present when signed values
cancel. Each round completes its returns and cross-returns before the next
round consumes them. This realizes the committed-input order of
\mainsection{subsec:duon-continuity}; the pacing and orientation weights are
declared finite-model choices.

After three inner rounds, the new outer certificate binds the extended inner
prefixes. An independent \(3{:}3\) reference executes RD-paced traversals with
a one-cut restart. Within the recorded window from the start of internal
execution to the new outer return, repeated bound cross-returns supply the
native cadence and lag comparison. Window endpoints depend on the particular
construction. A quotient of results from different windows therefore does not
by itself identify an atomic frequency ratio.

\paragraph{Specialization.}
The neutral hydrogen-1 construction has \(B=34\) and
\(RD_{\mathrm{outer}}=69\). The neutral Og-294 construction has 412 distinct
constituents, \(B=15\,980\) and \(RD_{\mathrm{outer}}=31\,961\).
Neither scalar alone specifies emitted radiation or isotope survival.
The finite campaign retains all simulated histories. Exact enumeration of a
small orientation tree checks its conditional probability mass; larger
inventories use a recorded integer sampler. A sampled trajectory is not an
ensemble survival curve.

\paragraph{Calibration.}
The historical hydrogen reductions in
\secref{manual2:hyperfine-application} remain separate from the generic
recurrence results. The SAO beat comparison consumes an emitted phase map and
the apparatus window; the SI anchor supplies the scale after the model ratio
has been identified. The source-specific Table IV, Table VI and combined-mean
uncertainties remain attached to their respective quantities.

For isotope survival, a configuration-dependent retention/loss observation
must first determine which executed event contributes to loss of the specified
state. A reference-recurrence calibration then supplies physical elapsed time.
The existing square-path survival fixture does not select that nuclear loss
rule or transfer its capacity to the 135 inventories.

\paragraph{Falsification.}
Matched feedback controls retain the first round and alter only subsequent
continuation. Omitting the observation projection preserves the executed
trajectory. Original-input
replay detects replaced returns, changed contacts and forged status promotion,
even when local hashes are recomputed. Ground/isomer labels without a distinct
preparation map cannot manufacture different dynamics. No state reduction is
used; a later reduction must preserve the joint conditional law of the next
reduced state and the emitted observable.
